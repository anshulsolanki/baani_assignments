# Non-Verbal & Multimodal Discourse Analysis Worksheet

*Qualitative analysis of pauses, trailing sentences, prosody/intonation, vocalisations (coughs, sighs, scoffs), and kinesics (hand gestures, facial expressions, posture) in political news discourse*

---

## Guidelines: Five non-verbal & paralinguistic features to look for in every event

| Feature | What to look for | Illustrative example (from sample) | Questions to answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Pauses, Silences & Abrupt Stops** | Micro-pauses `(.)`, timed silences `(1.5)`, pregnant/dramatic pauses before a loaded word, hesitant stumbles, abrupt self-interruptions or cut-offs (`—`), and forced silences (muting a panelist's mic or talking over them). | Anchor pauses `(1.5)` before saying *"'loota'"* for dramatic weight; Addl. DCP cuts off mid-sentence (*"wo toh— abhi investigation chal raha hai"*) when asked if police struck first; panelist's mic faded during counter-question. | Where does the pause or stop occur? Is it deliberate/rhetorical (for suspense, emphasis, or gravitas) or defensive/evasive (cognitive load, self-repair, avoiding blame)? Who controls turn-taking and silence? |
| **2. Trailing Sentences & Unfinished Utterances** | Clauses that trail off (`...`), drop in volume at the end, or leave the conclusion unspoken (aposiopesis) so the audience supplies the implication without the speaker taking explicit responsibility. | *"Aur phir wahan jo log ghuse the... aap samajh hi sakte hain..."*; *"500 salon ke sangharsh ke baad agar aisa..."* trailing off with a slow head shake. | What exact thought is left unfinished? What presupposition or shared bias is the viewer invited to fill in? Does trailing off act as a safe hedge to imply higher-level complicity or "anti-national" motive without stating it? |
| **3. Intonation, Pitch, Volume & Prosody** | Pitch contour (rising `↑` for incredulity/challenge; falling `↓` for finality), volume shifts (shouting vs. hushed whisper), emphatic word stress, speech rate (rapid-fire urgency vs. slow solemnity), and tonal register (sarcastic, reverential, combative). | Hushed, reverential cadence on *"Prabhu Shri Ram ke Mandir"* vs. sharp, high-pitched interrogation on *"par Akhilesh ji Ram Mandir kyon↑ nahin jate?!"*; satirical sing-song inflection on *"priventiv arenjment"*. | Which exact words receive pitch peaks or volume stress? How does vocal tone turn neutral words into irony, mockery, or moral outrage? Does the speaker shift prosodic register depending on which actor is discussed? |
| **4. Coughs, Sighs & Non-Lexical Vocalisations** | Audible sighs (`[sigh]`), scoffs/snorts (`[scoff]`), ironic chuckles (`[chuckle]`), throat-clearing/coughs (`[cough]`), sharp intakes of breath/gasps (`[gasp]`), and tongue-clicks (`[tsk]`). | Heavy `[sigh]` before reporting Trust oversight lapses (signalling reluctant sorrow rather than hostility); mocking `[scoff]` or `[chuckle]` when reading an opponent's denial; `[cough / throat-clear]` when pivoting away from an awkward fact. | What emotion or epistemic stance does the vocalisation perform (exasperation, moral disgust, disbelief, ridicule, discomfort)? Does a sigh or scoff pre-judge a speaker's claim before any verbal rebuttal is made? |
| **5. Hand Gestures, Facial Expressions & Posture (Kinesics)** | Hand movements (air-quotes, finger-pointing/wagging, desk-thumping, open palms, counting on fingers, dismissive wave), facial cues (raised eyebrows, eye-rolls, smirk, furrowed brow), head nods/shakes, leaning into camera, and visual props (bandaged limbs, documents). | Anchor forms physical air-quotes on *"'loota'"* or *"'peaceful' protest"*; host points finger/pen at lens during an ultimatum; injured DCP holds bandaged hand up to camera; creator raises one eyebrow and leans in for a conspiratorial reveal. | How do gestures reinforce, contradict, or sharpen the spoken words? During split-screen listening, does the host's face (nodding vs. smirking/eye-rolling) cue the audience on whom to trust? How are bodily visuals used as evidence? |

## Quick Multimodal Transcription Notation Key (use inside transcript excerpts)

| Symbol | Meaning | Example in transcript | Symbol | Meaning | Example in transcript |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `(.)` | Brief micro-pause (< 0.5 sec) | `woh (.) suparavijan lacking tha` | `↑ / ↓` | Marked rising / falling pitch | `kyon↑ FIR nahin karaee↓` |
| `(1.5)` | Timed pause in seconds | `kisne (1.5) 'loota'?` | `WORD / _word_` | Loud volume / emphatic stress | `ye PAAP hai / _bilkul_ galat` |
| `—` | Abrupt stop, cut-off, or self-repair | `police ki taraf se— abhi jaanch...` | `°word°` | Softened / whispered delivery | `°ander ki baat ye hai°` |
| `...` | Trailing off / unfinished sentence | `aap jaante hain ki kaun log...` | `[sigh] [cough] [scoff]` | Non-lexical vocalisations | `[sigh] 500 salon ke baad...` |
| `[overlap]` | Simultaneous / overlapping speech | `[Anjana: suniye—] [Guest: nahin!]` | `{{gesture: ...}}` | Hand gesture / facial cue / posture | `{{air-quotes on 'loota'}}` |

**Methodological Reminder:** A non-verbal cue is not automatically manipulative or ideological—a cough, pause, or rapid cadence may reflect studio audio delay, live time pressure, or a speaker's habitual style. Always check (1) whether the cue clusters with verbal features, and (2) whether the speaker's delivery shifts noticeably across different actors or events (within-outlet baseline).
