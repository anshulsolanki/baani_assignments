## 3. Fifteen Likely Viva Questions & Confident Model Answers

### Q1: What is the main difference between Phase 1 and Phase 2 of your Language and Society project?
* **Answer:** Phase 1 analysed the **verbal/lexical layer** of the transcripts—looking at verb choices, active vs. passive voice, naming labels, and presuppositions. Phase 2 analyses the **non-verbal, paralinguistic, and kinesic layer** of the video broadcasts—examining how pauses `(1.5)`, trailing sentences `...`, pitch/volume contours (`↑`, `↓`, `°word°`), vocalisations (`[sigh]`, `[scoff]`), and body language (`{{gesture: ...}}`) interact with those words to construct ideological meaning.

### Q2: How can a non-verbal cue completely invert the literal meaning of a spoken word in your dataset?
* **Answer:** Through **paralinguistic irony and kinesic scare-quoting**. For instance, when Aaj Tak (`Event 1`, `[04:15]`) and Republic TV (`Event 1`, `[00:25]`) utter the words *"'loota'"* or *"'ghotala'"*, they precede the word with a dramatic `(1.0)`–`(1.5)` pause, raise their pitch `↑` in mock incredulity, and form physical two-handed air-quotes `{{gesture: air-quotes}}`. Even though the word `"ghotala"` appears in the transcript, the non-verbal delivery tells the viewer the exact opposite: that no scam occurred and the allegation is absurd.

### Q3: What is "aposiopesis" (trailing sentences `...`), and why do news presenters use it?
* **Answer:** Aposiopesis is the rhetorical strategy of leaving a clause unfinished (`...`) so the listener supplies the implied conclusion. In political news, it serves as a **safe legal and editorial hedge**. For example, Dhruv Rathee (`Event 1`, `[06:32]`) names the BJP Mayor and Trustee as witnesses on the 5-minute land flip and trails off: *"ab isse zyada saaf saboot kya chahiye ki iske peechhe kaun log..."*. Similarly, Aaj Tak (`Event 2`, `[11:52]`) asks *"Kya ye waqai chhatra hain ya inke peechhe kisi siyasi party ka..."*. Neither speaker explicitly states the final libellous accusation, yet their trailing intonation and knowing eyebrow raise guide the audience to fill in the blank.

### Q4: Why is Mohak Mangal marked as "Skipped" for Event 1, and what does that tell us analytically?
* **Answer:** Per our official project guidelines (`lns_project_updated_guidelines.pdf`, page 1), Mohak Mangal did not make any video on the Ram Mandir donation/land scam (only a historical explainer on the Ayodhya dispute), so Event 1 is skipped. Analytically, this represents **editorial gatekeeping / structural silence**: unlike Dhruv Rathee or daily TV channels, Mohak's channel (*Soch*) avoids acute partisan flashpoints involving religious institutions, whereas in Event 2 (NEET paper leak) he produces a deep 20-minute policy and statistical explainer because education directly impacts his core youth demographic.

### Q5: How did you distinguish between a deliberate rhetorical pause and a defensive/evasive stop?
* **Answer:** By looking at syntax, prosody, and placement:
  * A **deliberate rhetorical pause** (`(1.0)`–`(1.5)`) occurs *before* a key numeral or punchline (like Sarthak pausing `(1.2)` before saying *"_67_ students got `720/720`"* at `[03:42]`) or *after* an opponent's clip (like *The Wire* pausing `(1.5)` after Champat Rai's refusal to answer at `[07:48]`), and is accompanied by steady eye contact.
  * A **defensive stop / self-repair** (`—`) breaks a syntactic phrase mid-stream when a speaker faces cognitive load or blame—such as the Additional DCP on Aaj Tak (`Event 2`, `[10:35]`) cutting himself off (*"Wo toh— abhi investigation chal raha hai"*) when asked if police struck students first, accompanied by gaze aversion and micro-pauses `(.)`.

### Q6: How do vocalisations like `[sigh]` and `[scoff]` function as epistemic stances?
* **Answer:** Non-lexical vocalisations pre-judge a claim before any verbal argument is made:
  * A **`[sigh]`** signals **reluctant sorrow or moral exhaustion**. On Aaj Tak (`Event 1`, `[02:30]`), Anjana sighs before mentioning the lack of frisking at the Temple Trust (*"[sigh] mann ko dukh hota hai"*), framing her critique as the sorrow of a devotee rather than hostility. On Mohak Mangal (`Event 2`, `[09:10]`), his weary `[sigh]` after the NTA press conference signals disappointed civic expectation.
  * A **`[scoff]` or ironic `[chuckle]`** signals **contempt and epistemic dismissal**. When Dhruv Rathee (`Event 2`, `[08:42]`) chuckles at the NTA appointing its own committee (*"chor hi chori ki jaanch karega"*), the scoff strips the committee of legitimacy instantly.

### Q9: Why is checking the "within-outlet speaker baseline" so important in multimodal analysis?
* **Answer:** Because every creator has a habitual genre style: TV anchors speak louder and faster (~160–185 WPM) due to live studio pressure, whereas YouTube policy explainers like Mohak Mangal speak calmly (~125 WPM) in a studio. If we only compared Arnab's volume to Mohak's volume, we would just be measuring **format differences** (TV debate vs. solo explainer). By checking **within-outlet shifts**—such as how Anjana's voice shifts between Act I (Temple Trust, ~115 WPM, hushed `°...°`) and Act II (Akhilesh Yadav, ~155 WPM, shouted `↑`) inside the exact same 8-minute bulletin—we isolate true ideological stance.

### Q11: How do solo YouTube explainers control "turn-taking" when they don't have live panelists to interrupt?
* **Answer:** Solo creators control turn-taking through **clip editing and post-clip reaction pauses**. When Dhruv Rathee (`Event 1`, `[11:48]`), Mohak Mangal (`Event 2`, `[09:08]`), or *The Wire* (`Event 1`, `[07:48]`) play an official denial clip (from Champat Rai or the NTA Director), they cut the clip right after the most evasive sentence and insert a **1.0- to 1.5-second silent reaction stare** (often paired with a raised eyebrow, slow head shake, or `[scoff]`). That silent reaction frame tells the viewer how to interpret the clip just as powerfully as a live interruption.
