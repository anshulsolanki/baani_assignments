## 1. Why Study the Non-Verbal & Multimodal Layer?

In **Phase 1**, we studied **what** news creators say—their verbs, nouns, active/passive voice, and framing metaphors. However, in broadcast television and YouTube video journalism, **how** something is said (and shown) can reinforce, soften, or completely invert the literal meaning of the words on the page.

For example, consider the spoken word `"loota"` (looted) in Event 1 or `"retaliation"` in Event 2:
* Written on a transcript page, `"loota"` looks like an allegation of theft.
* Spoken by **Anjana Om Kashyap (Aaj Tak)** after a `1.5`-second pause `(1.5)` with **two-handed physical air-quotes** `{{gesture: air-quotes on 'loota'}}` and a sharp rising pitch `↑`, the exact same word is transformed into a **mockery of the Opposition**, signalling to the audience that the allegation is fake news.
* Spoken by **Dhruv Rathee** with a **downward two-handed karate chop** `{{gesture: bilateral vertical hand-chop}}` and firm falling intonation `↓`, the word is performed as an **established forensic fact**.

This guide explains the **five non-verbal dimensions** we analysed in Phase 2 and synthesises our findings across all 6 creators.

## 2. Theoretical Framework: The Five Non-Verbal Features

### Feature 1: Pauses, Silences & Abrupt Stops (`(.)`, `(1.5)`, `—`, `[overlap]`)
In conversation analysis and sociolinguistics, silence is never "empty." It serves three distinct functions in political news:
1. **Rhetorical / Dramatic Pauses (`(1.0)`–`(1.5)`):** Used by solo explainers and anchors right before a shocking number or loaded word to build suspense (e.g., Sarthak Goswami pausing `(1.2)` before saying *"_SADSATH_ (`67`) students ko full `720` marks mile↑!"* at `[03:42]`), or used *after* playing an official denial clip so the official's evasion hangs awkwardly in the air (e.g., Dhruv Rathee and *The Wire* holding a `(1.5)` deadpan silence after Champat Rai's quote).
2. **Defensive / Evasive Stops & Self-Repairs (`—`):** Occur when a speaker realises mid-sentence that their words might incriminate their side, triggering an abrupt cut-off and lexical repair.
   * *Example 1:* On Aaj Tak (`Event 1`, `[02:38]`), Anjana starts saying *"Trust ki taraf se—"* (On the part of the Trust—), abruptly stops `—`, and repairs it to *"matlab jo counting agency aur bank ke log the..."* to shift blame away from the Temple Trust onto external bank clerks.
   * *Example 2:* On Aaj Tak (`Event 2`, `[10:35]`), when the Additional DCP is asked whether police lathi-charged students first, he stammers: *"Wo toh— (.) abhi investigation chal raha hai..."*
3. **Forced Silences & Acoustic Gatekeeping (`[overlap]` + Mic-Ducking):** In TV panel debates (Aaj Tak and Republic TV), the anchor controls institutional power by interrupting dissenting guests mid-sentence `[overlap]` and having the studio control room fade down the guest's microphone volume.

### Feature 2: Trailing Sentences & Unfinished Utterances (`...` / Aposiopesis)
**Aposiopesis** is the rhetorical device of suddenly breaking off a sentence and leaving the thought unfinished (`...`). In news discourse, trailing off acts as a **safe legal and ideological hedge**:
* The speaker plants all the premises of a serious accusation (such as top-down political corruption or an "anti-Hindu conspiracy") and then trails off with a raised eyebrow or head shake (*"aap khud samajh sakte hain ki iske peechhe kaun log..."*).
* Because the viewer mentally fills in the blank using their shared bias or presupposition, the persuasion is even stronger—yet the speaker can claim they never explicitly made the allegation.

### Feature 3: Intonation, Pitch, Volume & Prosody (`↑`, `↓`, `WORD`, `°word°`)
Prosody is the musicality and acoustic contour of speech:
* **Rising Pitch (`↑`):** Signals incredulity, sarcastic challenge, or open interrogation (e.g., Anjana asking *"Par Akhilesh ji Ram Mandir kyon↑ nahin jaate?!"* at `[05:18]`).
* **Falling Pitch (`↓`):** Signals finality, unshakeable certainty, and moral verdicts (e.g., Dhruv Rathee's *"Ye sab kuchh sarkari kagazon mein darj hai↓"* at `[05:12]`).
* **Softened / Whispered Delivery (`°word°`):** Signals either **sacred reverence** (`°Prabhu Shri Ram ke charnon mein°` on Aaj Tak), **intimate empathy for victims** (`°ek middle-class parivar apni poori jama-poonji laga deta hai°` on Mohak Mangal), or **conspiratorial stealth** (`°chupke se NEET ka result nikaal diya°` on Sarthak Goswami).

### Feature 4: Coughs, Sighs & Non-Lexical Vocalisations (`[sigh]`, `[scoff]`, `[chuckle]`, `[cough]`, `[gasp]`)
Non-lexical vocalisations are pre-verbal evaluations—they tell the viewer *how to feel* about a statement before a single word of rebuttal is spoken:
* **`[sigh]`:** Used by Aaj Tak (`Event 1`, `[02:30]`) to perform **reluctant devotional sorrow** when admitting counting lapses (*"mann ko dukh hota hai"*), signalling that the criticism comes from love rather than hostility. Conversely, used by Mohak Mangal (`Event 2`, `[09:10]`) and *The Wire* (`Event 2`, `[11:10]`) to perform **weary civic exhaustion** at the NTA's bureaucratic denials.
* **`[scoff]` / `[chuckle]`:** Used across almost all outlets as an **epistemic delegitimiser**—an instant acoustic dismissal that frames an opponent's claim as laughable.
* **`[cough / throat-clear]`:** Used as a **conversational pivot** when moving away from an uncomfortable question (e.g., Aaj Tak `[10:42]` pivoting away after the DCP stammers on who used force first).

### Feature 5: Hand Gestures, Facial Expressions & Posture (Kinesics & Visual Props)
* **Spatial & Enumerative Gestures (YouTube Explainers):** Dhruv Rathee, Mohak Mangal, and Sarthak Goswami rely heavily on **sequential finger-counting** (to make student demands and audit anomalies look structured and rational) and **spatial hand spans** (holding hands close together for `Rs 2 crore` and flinging them wide apart for `Rs 18.5 crore` to visualise the 825% markup).
* **Dominance & Dismissal Gestures (TV Debates):** Anjana Om Kashyap and Arnab Goswami use **index-finger wagging at the lens**, **pen-jabbing**, **desk-thumping**, and **backhanded dismissive waves** to assert courtroom dominance over panelists.
* **Visual Props of Injury (Event 2's Ultimate Contrast):**
  * **Aaj Tak (`[08:40]`):** Close-up of the Additional DCP holding his **white-bandaged hand** up to the camera to substantiate protester violence.
  * **The Wire (`[08:12]`):** Close-up of a student protester holding his **bruised, scraped wrist** up to the camera after being dragged into a police bus to substantiate state repression.

## 3. Methodological Safeguard: Within-Outlet Speaker Baselines

A central methodological rule in Phase 2 is that **a non-verbal cue is not automatically ideological**:
* Arnab Goswami shouts at ~180 WPM on almost every topic; Sarthak Goswami raises his eyebrows and cracks jokes on almost every topic; Mohak Mangal speaks calmly at ~125 WPM on almost every topic.
* Therefore, to prove ideological framing, we look for **within-speaker shifts across actors or sub-topics**:
  1. **Anjana Om Kashyap (Aaj Tak):** Shifts from a slow (~115 WPM), hushed `°...°`, sighing register with clasped hands when discussing the Temple Trust (`Event 1`, Act I) or the injured DCP (`Event 2`, `[08:48]`), to a rapid (~155 WPM), loud, finger-wagging, rising-pitch `↑` register when addressing Akhilesh Yadav (`Event 1`, Act II) or protesting students (`Event 2`, `[06:12]`).
  2. **Arnab Goswami (Republic TV):** Drops his trademark shouting in `Event 2` (`[07:10]`–`[11:45]`) to rest his chin on his hand and speak softly (`°Tell me, young man...°`) to an individual NEET aspirant, even holding up a vertical stop-palm to silence politicians—before surging back to `190 WPM` desk-thumping rage at `[12:00]` to blame the Bihar RJD Opposition for the paper leak.
  3. **Sarthak Goswami:** Maintains detached comedic irony throughout `Event 1` (Ram Mandir), but drops his comedic distance in the final third of `Event 2` (`[15:40]`–`[18:00]`) into a quiet, unsmiling register when discussing student mental health and suicides.
