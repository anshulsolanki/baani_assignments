## 1. Goal of the Project & Research Questions

### 1.1 Primary Goal
Biomedical research articles (such as those in *PLOS* and *eLife*) are long, syntactically dense, and filled with background methodology, making it difficult for patients and clinicians to extract core findings quickly. However, standard summarization approaches face critical limitations in the medical domain:
1. **Surface Statistical Baselines (`TF-IDF`, `TextRank`) Are Linguistically Blind:** Pure word-frequency (`TF-IDF`) and lexical-overlap graph methods (`TextRank`) ignore **discourse structure**, **syntactic clause nuclearity**, and **semantic roles**. As a result, they frequently extract background literature review sentences or methodology boilerplate that happen to repeat frequent medical terms, while missing the article's actual clinical conclusion.
2. **Pre-Trained Abstractive Models & LLMs Suffer from Hallucinations and Opacity:** Black-box neural models can hallucinate numeric dosages, invert negation scope (*"no significant adverse effect"* $\to$ *"significant adverse effect"*), and offer no linguistic interpretability.
3. **Need for a Linguistically Grounded, Trainable Summarization Pipeline:** As suggested in our proposal review with the instructor, a rigorous Computational Linguistics approach should **first build a rule-based linguistic summarizer** incorporating **Rhetorical Structure Theory (RST)**, **syntactic parsing**, and **semantic representations**, **then convert those linguistic rules into features for a Logistic Regression classifier**, and finally **guide and fine-tune the model using the best-performing linguistic features**.

The goal of this project is to **design, implement from scratch, and evaluate a 4-stage non-neural linguistic medical summarization pipeline** (`Rule-Based Linguistic Summarizer` $\to$ `Rules-to-Features Mapping` $\to$ `Logistic Regression & Naive Bayes Sentence Classifier` $\to$ `Feature-Guided Fine-Tuning & Extrinsic Symptoms-to-Disease Evaluation`) on the **BioLaySumm2025 (PLOS & eLife)** biomedical corpus.

### 1.2 Research Questions
1. **RQ1 (Rule-Based Linguistic Summarization vs. Surface Baselines):** How much does a rule-based extractive summarizer that integrates **Rhetorical Structure Theory (RST)** (Nucleus vs. Satellite weighting), **Syntactic Parsing** (clause completeness and appositive pruning), and **Semantic Representations** (predicate–argument triples and clinical concept density) improve `ROUGE-1/2/L` and factual reliability over surface `TF-IDF` and `TextRank` baselines?
2. **RQ2 (Rules-to-Features Logistic Regression & Feature Selection):** When we convert our linguistic rules into a structured feature vector to train a supervised **Logistic Regression** (and **Naive Bayes**) sentence extractor, which linguistic features receive the highest learned weights, and how much does **fine-tuning the model using the best-performing linguistic feature subset** improve summary quality?
3. **RQ3 (Factual Reliability & Extrinsic Symptoms-to-Disease Classification):** How effectively do summaries generated by our linguistically guided Logistic Regression model preserve **numeric clinical values**, **negation polarity**, and **diagnostic symptom-to-disease associations** (measured via an extrinsic non-neural **Symptoms-to-Disease classifier**) compared to standard baselines?

## 3. Scope of the Project

### 3.1 In-Scope
1. **Statistical & Graph Extractive Baselines (`M0`):**
   - Implementing sentence-level **TF-IDF** scoring and **TextRank** (PageRank over sentence cosine/lexical-overlap similarity graphs) from scratch in Python.
2. **Stage 1 — Rule-Based Linguistic Summarizer (`M1`):**
   - **Rhetorical Structure Theory (`RST`) Module:** Segmenting sentences into clauses/EDUs and classifying explicit discourse markers into *Conclusion/Result* (*"therefore", "consequently", "our results show"*), *Contrast* (*"however", "nevertheless"*), *Cause* (*"because", "due to"*), and *Concession* (*"although", "despite"*), upweighting **Nucleus** claims ($w_N > 1$) and downweighting **Satellite** background/concessions ($w_S < 1$).
   - **Syntactic Parser & Anaphora Module (`use a parser`):** Using NLTK POS tagging and CFG/syntactic chunking (`NP`, `VP`, `PP`, `SBAR`) to reward sentences with complete main-clause `[NP-Subj + VP + NP-Obj]` structures, penalize fragmentary/citation-heavy clauses, and apply **Hobbs'/Binding-Theory Anaphora Resolution** to penalize or repair sentences with unresolved initial pronouns (*"It", "They", "This"*).
   - **Meaning / Semantic Representation Module:** Representing each sentence as structured **Predicate–Argument Semantic Triples** (`<Subject_Entity, Predicate_Verb, Object_Entity, Polarity/Modality>`), counting domain-specific semantic concepts (**Symptoms, Diseases, Treatments, Outcomes** via WordNet + medical lexicons), and tracking **syntactic negation scope** (*no, not, neither, without*) and **numeric clinical evidence**.
3. **Stage 2 & 3 — Rules-to-Features Conversion & Supervised Logistic Regression (`M2`):**
   - Converting all 15 statistical, RST, syntactic-parser, and semantic-representation rules into a sentence feature vector $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{15}$.
   - Generating **Oracle Extractive Labels** $y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ on the training split (sentences that maximize ROUGE overlap with the gold summary) and training custom **L2-Regularized Logistic Regression** and **Naive Bayes** (Kupiec et al., 1995) classifiers from scratch.
4. **Stage 4 — Feature-Guided Fine-Tuning & Extrinsic `Symptoms-to-Disease` Evaluation (`M3`):**
   - Ranking learned Logistic Regression weights $|w_j|$, running group-wise feature ablation, and **fine-tuning the model using the best-performing linguistic features** (plus an RST/anaphora coherence post-processor).
   - Evaluating summaries using `ROUGE-1/2/L`, the **Python Factual Keyword & Numeric Regex Checker**, and an extrinsic **Symptoms-to-Disease Classification** benchmark.

### 3.2 Out-of-Scope
- Training or fine-tuning neural networks, Transformers, or LLMs (if pre-trained HuggingFace/LLM outputs are inspected at all, they serve strictly as an optional external reference point—100% of our core implementation is non-neural linguistic & statistical modeling).
- Manual human evaluation by medical professionals.

## 4. Datasets & Objective Evaluation Metrics

### 4.1 Datasets
1. **Primary Summarization Corpus — BioLaySumm2025 (`PLOS` & `eLife` Subsets):**
   - **Source:** `https://huggingface.co/datasets/BioLaySumm/BioLaySumm2025-PLOS` and `BioLaySumm/BioLaySumm2025-eLife` (loaded as structured JSON/CSV text splits; we use a stratified subset of **1,000 biomedical articles**: 800 train / 100 validation / 100 test so local CPU syntactic/semantic parsing runs cleanly and fast).
   - **Structure:** Each record contains the full biomedical article text, technical abstract, and gold-standard summary, alongside biomedical disease/symptom concepts and numeric clinical findings.
2. **Symptoms-to-Disease Classification Dataset (For Extrinsic Clinical Evaluation & Feature Lexicon):**
   - **Source:** Standard open **Symptom-to-Disease medical text dataset** (e.g., *Disease-Symptom Knowledge Database / Gretel / Kaggle Symptom2Disease* corpus mapping clinical symptom descriptions to disease categories, plus disease/symptom category labels extracted from the `BioLaySumm` articles).
   - **Role in Project:** Used both to build our **Medical Semantic Concept Lexicon** (Symptoms vs. Diseases vs. Treatments) and to run the **Extrinsic Symptoms-to-Disease Classifier** (Naive Bayes & Logistic Regression) that tests whether our generated summaries preserve diagnostic symptom-to-disease relationships.

### 4.2 Objective Evaluation Metrics (Zero Human Evaluation Required)
1. **Intrinsic Overlap Metrics:**
   - **ROUGE-1** (unigram recall/precision/F1), **ROUGE-2** (bigram overlap), and **ROUGE-L** (Longest Common Subsequence sentence-level structural overlap), implemented in Python.
2. **Python Rule-Based Factual & Semantic Reliability Checker:**
   - **Keyword & Semantic Triple Coverage (%):** Proportion of gold-summary medical entities (Symptoms, Diseases, Treatments) and `<Subject, Predicate, Object>` semantic triples retained in the generated summary.
   - **Numeric & Statistical Evidence Recall (%):** Regex-based verification of numeric values, dosages (`mg, ml, %`), sample sizes (`n = ...`), and statistical significance markers (`p < ...`, confidence intervals).
   - **Negation Polarity Accuracy (%):** Checks whether negated findings in the source sentence retain their negation marker in the extracted summary.
3. **Extrinsic Clinical Utility — Symptoms-to-Disease Classification Accuracy & Macro-F1:**
   - A trained **Naive Bayes / Logistic Regression Symptoms-to-Disease Classifier** is evaluated on: (a) Full Article / Gold Summary, (b) `TF-IDF` / `TextRank` summaries, (c) Rule-Based Linguistic summaries (`M1`), and (d) Fine-Tuned Feature-Guided Logistic Regression summaries (`M3`). Higher classification F1 proves that the summary preserved the essential diagnostic semantics!

### Detailed 15-Feature Vector ($\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{15}$) Converted from Linguistic Rules:
1. **Statistical & Positional Rules (`f1`–`f4`):**
   - `f1_tfidf_salience`: Mean TF-IDF score of lemmatized content words in sentence $s_i$.
   - `f2_textrank_centrality`: PageRank centrality score of $s_i$ in the article's lexical-overlap graph.
   - `f3_section_position`: Normalized discourse position (rewarding Introduction/Conclusion & section-initial/final topic sentences; Kupiec et al., 1995).
   - `f4_length_regularization`: Penalty for overly short fragments ($<6$ tokens) or overly long table/citation dumps.
2. **Rhetorical Structure Theory (`RST`) Discourse Rules (`f5`–`f8`):**
   - `f5_rst_nucleus_score`: Indicator/weight if $s_i$ is tagged as an RST **Nucleus** clause.
   - `f6_rst_satellite_penalty`: Indicator if $s_i$ is dominated by a concessive/background **Satellite** (*although, despite, whereas*).
   - `f7_conclusion_result_relation`: Presence of causal/result rhetorical connectives (*therefore, consequently, we conclude, findings indicate*).
   - `f8_contrastive_claim_relation`: Presence of antithesis/contrast connectives introducing a core finding (*however, in contrast, notably*).
3. **Syntactic Parser & Anaphora Rules (`f9`–`f11`):**
   - `f9_syntactic_svo_completeness`: CFG parser verification of a complete finite main clause (`[NP-Subj] + [VP-Finite] + [NP/PP-Obj]`).
   - `f10_subordination_ratio`: Ratio of main-clause tokens to embedded subordinate/parenthetical (`SBAR`/`PRN`) tokens.
   - `f11_anaphora_coherence_score`: Penalty if $s_i$ begins with an unresolved third-person pronoun or demonstrative (*"It", "They", "This"*) without a local antecedent, mitigated by our **Anaphora Resolution** repair rule.
4. **Meaning / Semantic Representation Rules (`f12`–`f15`):**
   - `f12_semantic_triple_density`: Number of clinical `<Agent/Treatment, Predicate, Symptom/Disease>` triples extracted from the sentence's parse tree.
   - `f13_symptom_disease_concept_count`: Density of verified **Symptom**, **Disease**, and **Therapeutic** concepts mapped via WordNet and our Symptom–Disease lexicon.
   - `f14_numeric_clinical_evidence`: Regex detection of quantitative experimental evidence (percentages, dosages, sample sizes, $p$-values).
   - `f15_factual_vs_irrealis_modality`: Distinguishes factual clinical outcomes (including explicit clinical negations like *"did not cause toxicity"*) from speculative/irrealis modals (*"might potentially", "future work could"*).

## 7. Expected Challenges & Mitigation Plan

| Expected Challenge | Impact | Concrete Mitigation Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Biomedical Articles Have Only Abstractive Gold Summaries (No Sentence-Level Labels)** | Supervised Logistic Regression requires binary labels $y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ for each sentence $s_i$. | **Mitigation:** Use the standard NLP **Greedy Oracle Extractive Labeling** algorithm (Nallapati et al., 2017; Kupiec et al., 1995): label the subset of source sentences that maximizes sentence-level `ROUGE-1 + ROUGE-2` against the gold summary as positive ($y_i = 1$) and the rest as negative ($y_i = 0$). |
| **2. Dangling Pronouns in Extracted Sentences** | Extracting a high-scoring sentence like *"It significantly reduced mortality by 34%"* loses meaning if the antecedent of *"It"* was in the previous sentence. | **Mitigation:** Integrate our CL2 **Anaphora Resolution module** (Hobbs' CFG algorithm + Binding Theory filters): when an extracted sentence contains an initial pronoun (`it, they, this drug`), automatically substitute the resolved antecedent NP (or include the antecedent's clause). |
| **3. Feature Collinearity & Overfitting in Rule-to-Feature Conversion** | Some linguistic features (e.g., `TF-IDF` and `symptom_disease_concept_count`) may be partially correlated. | **Mitigation:** Train Logistic Regression with **$L_2$ regularization ($\lambda$)**, compare against Naive Bayes, and perform **Stage 4 Feature Ablation & Selection** on the validation split to retain only the best-performing orthogonal linguistic features. |
| **4. Computational Runtime on Long Biomedical Articles** | Full `PLOS` articles can exceed 4,000 words, slowing down pairwise graph construction and CFG parsing. | **Mitigation:** Filter out non-prose sections (Reference lists, Acknowledgements, Author Contributions) during preprocessing and evaluate on a clean, stratified 1,000-article benchmark split. |
