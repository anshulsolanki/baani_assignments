\section*{Guidelines: Five features to look for in every event}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.18\textwidth} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.21\textwidth} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.23\textwidth} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.29\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Feature} & \textbf{What to look for} & \textbf{Illustrative example} & \textbf{Questions to answer} \\
\midrule
\textbf{1. Naming} & What are people, groups, actions, or events called? & ``Students'', ``protesters'', ``agitators''; ``irregularity'', ``scam'', ``controversy''. & What exact label is used? Who or what does it refer to? Who uses it? What alternative label could have been used? \\[6pt]
\textbf{2. Presupposition} & What is treated as already true or taken for granted? & ``Why are students creating chaos again?'' may presuppose earlier disruption. & What does the wording assume? Is that assumption supported, disputed, or left unexamined? Does it appear in a question or words such as ``again'', ``still'', or ``finally''? \\[6pt]
\textbf{3. Agency} & Who is shown doing something? Who is acted upon? Who disappears? & ``Police injured protesters'' vs.\ ``Protesters were injured.'' & Who is the actor? Who experiences the action? Is responsibility named, backgrounded, or omitted? \\[6pt]
\textbf{4. Antagonisation} & Who is positioned against whom? & ``Ordinary citizens'' vs.\ ``political troublemakers''. & Which groups are contrasted? Is one presented as more legitimate, moral, patriotic, dangerous, or disloyal? Is an ``us vs.\ them'' division implied? \\[6pt]
\textbf{5. Intensification / softening} & What is exaggerated, minimised, softened, or euphemised? & ``Violent mob'' vs.\ ``peaceful demonstrators''; ``incident'' instead of a direct description. & Which words heighten emotion or seriousness? Which minimise it? Are metaphors, dramatic adjectives, hedges, or euphemisms used? Does the wording match the evidence? \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent{\small\emph{Reminder:} A label or implication is not automatically false or manipulative. Examine the surrounding context and evidence.}

\noindent{Focus: How is the controversy framed---as financial accountability, religious sentiment, political motivation, or something else? Be precise about the specific allegation or episode covered.}

\noindent\textbf{Outlet:} Sarthak Goswami (\emph{The Sunday Show}, YouTube) \hfill \textbf{Date:} 2026-07-05
\formrule
\noindent\textbf{Video title:} \emph{Ram Mandir Scam, Gadkari's Ethanol, Kejriwal's Temple \& More | Sunday Show}
\formrule
\noindent\textbf{Timestamp(s):} Relevant Event 1 segment [00:00--06:30] (with brief callbacks at [27:55--28:15] and [29:15--29:20]; total video duration 45:41; unrelated weekly news segments [06:31--45:41] and Odoo POS sponsor segment [07:56--10:28] excluded per project guidelines. Contextual follow-up in 26 July episode EM1GeZufO6E at [27:17--29:45] also noted).
\formrule
\noindent\textbf{Analyst:} Baani Solanki (2025114016) \hfill \textbf{Language:} Hindi / Hinglish (Native Hindi audio, Romanized script; non-AI-dubbed)

\subsection*{Event-specific questions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=6pt, topsep=4pt]
  \item What terms are used: ``scam'', ``alleged irregularity'', ``corruption'', ``political controversy'', or something else?\par\smallskip
  Sarthak uses blunt vernacular terms of financial crime---ghotala [scam] ([00:00], [00:19], [00:46]), paisa chori / chanda chori [donation theft] ([00:00], [00:19], [00:29]), ghapla [bungling/fraud] ([02:21]), and skaimon [scams] ([01:31])---before reframing the controversy metaphorically as asli ghotala [the real scam] ([00:10], [04:04], [04:30], [05:22], [06:16]) and Ram ka charitra chori [theft of Ram's character/ethos] ([06:26]). He also coins the satirical political hybrid ``kangresiphikeshan of BJP politiks'' [Congressification of BJP politics] ([00:29]).
  \item How are the temple, trust, donors, and people making allegations described?\par\smallskip
  \textbf{Trust \& Accused Staff:} Senior trustee Champat Rai is labelled ``RSS VHP ka aadmi'' [an RSS-VHP man] ([00:46]) who claims innocence by blaming his driver ``salman khaan se inspireshan le rahe hain'' [taking inspiration from Salman Khan's hit-and-run defense] ([01:31--01:40]). Driver Ram Shankar ``Tinnu'' Yadav is simultaneously cited as the alleged ``mastarmaind'' ([00:37]) and satirized as ``bali ka bakra'' [scapegoat] ([00:46]). Counting staff like Lavkush Mishra are mocked for treating temple donations as ``hamare baap ka maal'' [our father's property] ([01:14]).

  \textbf{Donors / Public:} Devotees are depicted with biting second-person irony---both as ``bhakt bechare kamphyooj ho gae hain'' [poor devotees are confused about how to accuse one 'god' of stealing from another God] ([01:48--01:56]) and as complicit citizens (``aap bhi bhagidar ho usmen'', [02:29]; ``dharma chalane ka bachane ka theka toh tum hi ne diya tha'', [02:46--02:54]) who outsourced their religion to political contractors. Online hyper-partisans defending the theft are mocked for arguing ``Hindu ka paisa agar kisi Hindu ne le bhi liya... galat kya hai?'' ([02:54--03:12]).

  \textbf{People Making Allegations:} Local institutional actors like the Ayodhya ``baar esosieshan'' [Bar Association] threatening a city blockade ([01:48]) are cited alongside ``apojishan ne alag baval kaat diya hai'' [the opposition has raised its own ruckus] ([01:48]).
  \item Is the issue framed as financial accountability, an attack on Hindu faith, a political dispute, or a matter of public trust?\par\smallskip
  Primarily framed as \textbf{moral-civic accountability and the commercialization/politicization of religion (``dharma ka dhandha'', [02:21])}. Rather than treating the controversy as an external attack on Hindu faith, Sarthak argues that Hindu faith was hollowed out \emph{from within} by self-styled religious gatekeepers (``adharmi log jab dharma ka prachar karne lage wo tha asli ghotala'', [04:04]).
  \item Are questions about donations presented as legitimate scrutiny or as an attempt to malign the temple or its supporters?\par\smallskip
  Presented unequivocally as \textbf{legitimate, overdue scrutiny}. In fact, Sarthak ridicules partisan apologists who gaslight questioners (``tumhare dharma ka theka leke tumko hi loot liya aur ab tum sawal karoge'', [03:12--03:20]).
  \item Are allegations attributed to specific people and supported with evidence, or presented as established facts?\par\smallskip
  Hybrid: Financial recoveries and arrests are anchored to the SIT investigation (``SIT ki jaanch baithi jinhonne apni report dayar ki jiske baad Trust ke aath logon ko arest kiya gaya'', [00:29--00:37]) with concrete figures (Rs.\,20 lakh at Avinash Shukla's yoga center, Rs.\,12,000 salary vs Rs.\,25 lakh car and Rs.\,12 lakh stable for Lavkush Mishra at [01:14--01:23]). However, his broader claim that senior figures used the driver as a ``bali ka bakra'' ([00:46]) is presented through satirical inference (``ab aap thoda Google karo pata lag jaega'', [00:46]) rather than adjudicated proof.
  \item How are denials and counterarguments represented: explained, dismissed, challenged, or omitted?\par\smallskip
  \textbf{Mocked and challenged through satire.} Champat Rai's denial (``champat raay ji ka kahna hai ki ji main toh beksoor hoon. mere draivar ne mujhe dhokha diya hai'', [01:31--01:40]) is immediately ridiculed via the Salman Khan driver-blame trope ([01:40]). Partisan online counterarguments (``if a Hindu took a Hindu's money, why are you upset?'') are quoted and dismantled through reductio ad absurdum (``kal koi goli maar dega tumhen aur yeh kahenge ki Hindu ka khoon Hindu ne bahaya'', [03:12]).
  \item Does the presenter distinguish criticism of the trust or its financial practices from criticism of the temple or religion itself?\par\smallskip
  \textbf{Yes, explicitly and sharply.} He contrasts the ancient tradition of Hinduism (``hajaron lakhon saal purana dharma'', [03:38]) and Lord Ram's moral character against opportunistic political contractors and online abusers (``jo Mandir ke bahar chappal churate the unko andar ka theka de diya tumne... Ram ka chanda nahin Ram ka charitra chori ho chuka hai'', [06:16--06:26]).
  \item Who is portrayed as protecting donors' interests, and who as exploiting religious sentiment?\par\smallskip
  \textbf{Exploiting religious sentiment:} Trust insiders, VIP-darshan profiteers ([02:29--02:38]), BJP-RSS-linked contractors who peddled the ``Hindu khatre mein hai'' [Hindus are in danger] tonic ([03:20--03:28]), and abusive online ``dharma rakhvala'' trolls ([04:30--05:22]).

  \textbf{Protecting donors' interests:} No political party is idealized as a savior (later at [26:44--27:55] he equally mocks Kejriwal/AAP as ``sasti BJP'' for temple-appeasement politics); instead, Sarthak calls upon ordinary Hindu citizens themselves to wake up (``Hindu jago'', [06:26]) and reclaim ethical accountability.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Event-specific questions}
{\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=6pt, topsep=4pt]
  \item Are participants called ``students'', ``candidates'', ``protesters'', ``agitators'', ``mobs'', or ``political pawns''?\par\smallskip
  Sarthak consistently legitimizes participants as ``students'' ([00:08]), ``aam janta'' [ordinary public] ([03:39]), ``jenjis'' / Gen-Z youth ([10:40], [12:04]), ``lakhon bachche'' [lakhs of children/kids] ([22:48]), and ``lidarles moovment'' whose leader became ``khud pablik'' [the public itself] ([08:08]). He explicitly quotes and mocks mainstream TV anchors who label them ``aatankvadi'' [terrorists] ([05:31], [22:22]), ``goons oph da CJP'' ([05:38]), ``desh virodhi gaing'' [anti-national gang] ([05:47]), and ``pakistan phanded'' ([22:48]). He also honestly notes peripheral ``ansoshal eliments'' [anti-social elements] hooting near Connaught Place ([07:12--07:21]), separating them from the core Jantar Mantar site.
  \item Are their stated demands explained, summarised, mocked, questioned, or left out?\par\smallskip
  \textbf{Clearly explained, summarised, and endorsed.} He opens with Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan's resignation ([00:08]) and itemizes the remaining three accepted demands at [08:16--08:35]: (i) Rs.\,1 crore compensation to families of students who died by suicide due to paper leaks, (ii) a formal Delhi Police apology for the 20 July crackdown, and (iii) withdrawal/non-filing of criminal cases against protesters, alongside deeper systemic anti-leak reforms ([16:25--17:07]).
  \item Does the presenter focus on the original grievance, disruption, political affiliations, or public inconvenience?\par\smallskip
  Focuses squarely on the \textbf{original systemic grievance} (repeated paper leaks, student suicides, collapsing education budget from 4.5\% to 2.5\% of GDP, [15:59--18:10]) and \textbf{democratic civic solidarity} ([07:21--08:16]), while debunking claims of foreign funding ([00:40--00:48]).
  \item Who is represented as responsible: examination authorities, government, students, political parties, or others?\par\smallskip
  Primary responsibility is assigned to the \textbf{Union \& State Governments} (for reactive ``ditrens baay phiyar'' instead of preventive exam reforms and for slashing education spending in favor of electoral ``phri bis'' [freebies], [16:34--18:53]), \textbf{Delhi Police \& unbadged plainclothes men} for the 20 July crackdown ([03:39--04:17]), \textbf{Central Universities} (DU, JNU, Jamia, IITs) for issuing circulars threatening students with legal action ([15:00--15:20]), and \textbf{mainstream TV anchors} for vilifying youth ([05:31--05:56], [21:56--23:30]).
  \item Are questions framed around whether students have legitimate grievances, or why they are causing disruption?\par\smallskip
  Framed entirely around \textbf{students' legitimate grievances and state accountability}: why it took a massive street movement for a single resignation ([01:51], [15:59--16:16]), who ordered unbadged men to assault female protesters ([03:57--04:17]), and why education budgets are shrinking ([17:07--18:44]).
  \item Are claims of violence, disorder, or political manipulation attributed to sources and substantiated?\par\smallskip
  Claims of foreign/political conspiracy are refuted by citing the \textbf{Ministry of External Affairs (MEA)} clarification that there was ``koi bhi pakistan, chaina ya phoren phanding nahin'' ([00:40--00:48]). Claims of police violence (lathi charj, aansoo gais, pelet gans, plainclothes harassment at [03:39--04:17]) are backed by ground eyewitness observation ([07:12--08:16]) and cellphone videos ([04:53]), while fairly acknowledging that ``wahan pe kaee sare police vale bhi hart hue the. ritalieshan mein un pe bhi hamla huaa tha'' ([04:45--04:53]). He also transparently notes CJP founder Abhijit Dipke's prior AAP social-media stint ([02:19]).
  \item How are police and government actions described compared with protesters' actions? Are similar actions evaluated differently?\par\smallskip
  Police actions on 18 July and 20 July are condemned in stark active verbs: ``abadakt karke le jati hai. not diten abadakt'' [abducts, not detains Sonam Wangchuk] ([03:02--03:12]), ``broot phors yooz karke'' ([04:27]), ``pelet gans ki bauchhar'' ([03:39--03:47]). Conversely, protester clashes are contextualized as ``ritalieshan'' [retaliation] ([04:45]), and post-20-July protesters are celebrated for peaceful mutual aid---forming human chains, medical camps, book libraries, and Zomato/Swiggy food sharing ([07:21--08:08]). Interestingly, at [11:54--12:31], he humanizes young Gen-Z police jawans on the ground as sympathetic peers caught under senior orders.
  \item Are students allowed to explain their demands in their own words, or does the presenter mainly speak for them?\par\smallskip
  The presenter \textbf{mainly speaks for and narrates on behalf of the students} in his studio monologue format, supplemented by reading out student protest posters (``pavar of pipal ij gretar den da pipal in pavar'', [12:39]) and recounting his own visit to Jantar Mantar ([07:12--08:16]), while inserting brief clips of politicians and rival TV anchors ([05:38--05:56], [22:30]).
  \item Does the outlet's framing differ between coverage of the NEET paper-leak issue before the protest and coverage once students begin protesting?\par\smallskip
  Rather than turning hostile once students hit the streets (as mainstream TV channels did), Sarthak argues that the street protest \textbf{elevated and validated} his prior anti-corruption stance on education---declaring ``bhaad mein gaee nyootraliti. agar tum ejukeshan karapt kar doge toh desh barbad hai'' ([18:17--18:26]). \emph{(Note: Direct pre-protest episodes are outside this single transcript, though he references his continuous week-long coverage at [32:36].)}
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Guidelines: Five features to look for in every event}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.18\textwidth} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.21\textwidth} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.23\textwidth} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.29\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Feature} & \textbf{What to look for} & \textbf{Illustrative example} & \textbf{Questions to answer} \\
\midrule
\textbf{1. Naming} & What are people, groups, actions, or events called? & ``Students'', ``protesters'', ``agitators''; ``irregularity'', ``scam'', ``controversy''. & What exact label is used? Who or what does it refer to? Who uses it? What alternative label could have been used? \\[6pt]
