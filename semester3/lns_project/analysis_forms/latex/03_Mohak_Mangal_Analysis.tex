\section*{Guidelines: Five features to look for in every event}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.18\textwidth} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.21\textwidth} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.23\textwidth} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.29\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Feature} & \textbf{What to look for} & \textbf{Illustrative example} & \textbf{Questions to answer} \\
\midrule
\textbf{1. Naming} & What are people, groups, actions, or events called? & ``Students'', ``protesters'', ``agitators''; ``irregularity'', ``scam'', ``controversy''. & What exact label is used? Who or what does it refer to? Who uses it? What alternative label could have been used? \\[6pt]
\textbf{2. Presupposition} & What is treated as already true or taken for granted? & ``Why are students creating chaos again?'' may presuppose earlier disruption. & What does the wording assume? Is that assumption supported, disputed, or left unexamined? Does it appear in a question or words such as ``again'', ``still'', or ``finally''? \\[6pt]
\textbf{3. Agency} & Who is shown doing something? Who is acted upon? Who disappears? & ``Police injured protesters'' vs.\ ``Protesters were injured.'' & Who is the actor? Who experiences the action? Is responsibility named, backgrounded, or omitted? \\[6pt]
\textbf{4. Antagonisation} & Who is positioned against whom? & ``Ordinary citizens'' vs.\ ``political troublemakers''. & Which groups are contrasted? Is one presented as more legitimate, moral, patriotic, dangerous, or disloyal? Is an ``us vs.\ them'' division implied? \\[6pt]
\textbf{5. Intensification / softening} & What is exaggerated, minimised, softened, or euphemised? & ``Violent mob'' vs.\ ``peaceful demonstrators''; ``incident'' instead of a direct description. & Which words heighten emotion or seriousness? Which minimise it? Are metaphors, dramatic adjectives, hedges, or euphemisms used? Does the wording match the evidence? \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent{\small\emph{Reminder:} A label or implication is not automatically false or manipulative. Examine the surrounding context and evidence.}

\subsection*{Event-specific questions}
{\small
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=6pt, topsep=4pt]
  \item Are participants called ``students'', ``candidates'', ``protesters'', ``agitators'', ``mobs'', or ``political pawns''?\par\smallskip
  Mohak and his ground interviewees consistently identify participants as ``students'' ([00:10], [01:03], [03:54]), ``bachchon'' / ``10tha klas ka bachcha'' / ``16 se 22 saal ke ladkon'' [children / 10th-class child / 16-to-22-year-old boys] ([01:57--02:06], [04:30], [04:46--05:04]), ``ladakiyan'' / ``aantiyon'' / ``ledi'' [girls / aunties / lady] ([01:57], [02:51], [04:56]), ``net eksapiritent'' [NEET aspirant] ([04:30]), ``risentli grejueted'' youth ([08:16]), and ``sitijans'' / ``We the people of India'' ([Title], [09:10]). Crucially, Mohak intercuts \textbf{uncommented audio clips of ruling-camp politicians, judges, and TV anchors} calling them ``tukde-tukde'', ``dahashat gardon ka bi tim'' [B-team of terrorists] ([00:44--00:53]), ``goons [gsa] oph da CJP'' ([01:03], [10:56]), ``yangastars laik kokroch'' [youngsters like cockroaches] ([02:59]), and ``not students... CJP sponsard gundaj'' ([06:06])---letting the visual/verbal contrast expose those smears without shouting back.
  \item Are their stated demands explained, summarised, mocked, questioned, or left out?\par\smallskip
  \textbf{Articulated directly by the protesters, parents, and teachers themselves:} fair, leak-free exams after repeated 2024--2026 leaks ([03:27--04:12], [05:14]), accountability for 98 leaked papers over 10 years and student suicides ([05:14], [06:34]), flawed CET/NEET score normalization (630/800 reduced to 530 at [08:44--09:05]), and ministerial listening/accountability from Dharmendra Pradhan (``Dharmendra Pradhan mast lisan too as end aavar gavarnament ij obligeted too lisan too its sitijans'', [09:10--09:18]).
  \item Does the presenter focus on the original grievance, disruption, political affiliations, or public inconvenience?\par\smallskip
  Focuses deeply on the \textbf{human and constitutional cost of the original grievance}---4-year repeaters losing seats ([03:27--04:12]), mothers fearing student suicides ([06:14--07:01]), schoolteachers unable to teach the NCERT ``demokresi chaiptar'' ([07:14--07:58]), brain drain abroad ([07:32--07:40]), and future patient safety from ``doktar aaj phek'' [fake doctors buying leaked papers] ([07:40--07:58])---alongside \textbf{police breach of minimum-force guidelines} ([02:06--02:59]).
  \item Who is represented as responsible: examination authorities, government, students, political parties, or others?\par\smallskip
  \textbf{Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan and the Union Government} (for hypocrisy, denying leaks via ``deyar ij no evidens oph paper leak too last sevan iyars'' at [05:04], monologue governance via ``man ki baat'' instead of listening to youth at [05:14--05:47], and dividing citizens via ``Hindu muslim'' rhetoric at [05:47--05:57]), and \textbf{Delhi Police} (for removing nameplates and violating official lathi-charge guidelines by striking heads, eyes, and collarbones behind closed exit gates at [01:38--02:59], [04:38--05:04], [09:56--10:06]).
  \item Are questions framed around whether students have legitimate grievances, or why they are causing disruption?\par\smallskip
  Framed entirely around \textbf{legitimate citizen grievances, state hypocrisy, and democratic accountability}: e.g., ``toh ye sarkar itna bachchon se darti kyon hai?'' [why is this government so afraid of children?] ([04:30]), ``main ej a tichar demokresi chaiptar padha rahe ho. so vhat aaee tich student?'' ([07:14]), and Supriya's pointed question ``agar wo gilt mein nahin hai toh kyon itne kaimre hain? kyon logon ko hataya ja raha hai?'' ([10:17--10:27]).
  \item Are claims of violence, disorder, or political manipulation attributed to sources and substantiated?\par\smallskip
  \textbf{Police violence} is substantiated through \textbf{first-person injured eyewitnesses showing/describing wounds on camera} (leg blows from a ``thri star vala'' without a nameplate at [01:38--01:57], a 20-year-old woman's torn shoulder at [02:51], torn clothes of female protesters and an eye injury at [04:56--05:04], beating behind closed exit doors at [09:46--10:06]) measured directly against verbatim \textbf{Indian Police Minimum-Force Guidelines} read out by Mohak at [02:06--02:39]. Meanwhile, state/media claims of ``tukde-tukde'', ``dahashat gardon ka bi tim'' ([00:44]), ``brootal vaylens kairid aaut baay da [goons] oph da CJP'' ([01:03], [10:56]), and ``CJP sponsard gundaj'' ([06:06]) are played verbatim as \textbf{ironic counter-clips} immediately refuted by the next speaker on screen (e.g., [06:06] ``diz aar not students... diz aar CJP sponsard gundaj'' cuts straight to [06:14] a mother of two 10th/4th-grade daughters explaining why she came to sit for all India's children!).
  \item How are police and government actions described compared with protesters' actions? Are similar actions evaluated differently?\par\smallskip
  Mohak's opening masterstroke ([00:00--01:03]) compares \textbf{the exact same action across two eras}: in 1997, when 1,500 students marched to the Bhubaneswar Secretariat over an Odisha paper leak and suffered brutal police lathi-charge fracturing young ABVP National Secretary ``mukkoo bhaee'' (Dharmendra Pradhan), he was a heroic student victim; in 2026, ``dait da sem gaay jo 1997 mein paper leak ke khilaph protest kar rahe the. aaj unke khilaph protest ho rahi hai aur unki sarkar ab students par lathi charj kar rahi hai'' ([00:53--01:03]). Protesters in 2026 are consistently depicted as ``pisaphul'' / ``shantipoorn'' ([00:10], [04:38], [05:57], [10:36]) following ``satya aur ahinsa ka marg'' ([10:27--10:36]), whereas police action is ``inahyooman'' ([10:06]) and unlawful ([02:16--02:51]).
  \item Are students allowed to explain their demands in their own words, or does the presenter mainly speak for them?\par\smallskip
  \textbf{Students and ordinary citizens speak overwhelmingly in their own words!} Mohak himself speaks for \textbf{less than 2 minutes} of the entire 11:09 runtime ([00:00--00:44], [00:53--01:03], [02:06--02:59], and brief interviewer prompts at [01:12], [04:21], [08:44], [09:10]). Over \textbf{80\% of the video} consists of uninterrupted, raw voices of an injured male protester ([01:12--02:06]), a female 4-year NEET repeater ([03:18--04:21]), a Hyderabad hospital worker whose brother is a NEET aspirant ([04:21--05:04]), an anguished citizen critiquing ``man ki baat'' and communal polarization ([05:14--06:05]), a mother of two schoolgirls ([06:14--07:08]), a civics teacher ([07:14--07:58]), recent graduates ([08:16--09:18]), and Supriya from Konkan, Maharashtra ([09:29--10:56]).
  \item Does the outlet's framing differ between coverage of the NEET paper-leak issue before the protest and coverage once students begin protesting?\par\smallskip
  Rather than shifting from sympathy (on paper leaks) to alarmism (once street protests begin), Mohak treats the street protest as the \textbf{authentic embodiment of the Constitutional Preamble (``We the people of India...'')}, arguing through his interviewees that when 98 papers leak and institutional channels ignore youth for 25 days ([05:14--05:38]), peaceful street assembly is democracy's essential safeguard ([04:46], [09:10--09:18], [10:27--10:56]).
\end{enumerate}
}

\subsection*{Short summary}
\noindent{\small\textbf{What interpretation of the event does this video encourage?}}\par\smallskip
\noindent{\small That the 20 July Jantar Mantar protest was a deeply legitimate, peaceful, pan-Indian constitutional gathering (``We the people of India...'') of wronged NEET aspirants, anxious parents, and educators, brutally assaulted in violation of police manuals by a government led by an Education Minister who had himself been lathi-charged protesting a paper leak in 1997 ([00:00--01:03], [02:06--02:59]), while TV channels slandered ordinary mothers and teenagers as ``gundaj'' ([06:06--06:34]).}
\formrule
\noindent{\small\textbf{Whose perspective is foregrounded, challenged, or missing?}}\par\smallskip
\noindent{\small \emph{Foregrounded:} First-person voices of injured students, repeat NEET candidates, out-of-state family members (Hyderabad, Konkan), mothers, and schoolteachers ([01:12--10:56]). \emph{Challenged:} Dharmendra Pradhan's moral consistency ([00:00--01:03], [05:04]), police claims of lawful minimum force ([02:06--02:59]), the ``cockroach'' slur against unemployed youth ([02:59]), and TV ``goon/terrorist'' labels ([00:44], [01:03], [06:06], [10:56]). \emph{Missing:} Any interview with Delhi Police officers injured at barricades or detailed discussion of CJP's organizational leadership/political visitors.}
\formrule
\noindent{\small\textbf{What evidence supports your interpretation? What complicates it?}}\par\smallskip
\noindent{\small \emph{Supports:} Archival 1997 photograph/biography of Dharmendra Pradhan ([00:00--00:44]), verbatim Indian Police crowd-dispersal guidelines ([02:06--02:39]), concrete NEET/CET grievance details (2024 \& 2026 leaks at [03:37], 630 normalized to 530 at [08:54]), and raw on-site interviews with visibly shaken/injured citizens ([01:12--10:56]). \emph{Complicates:} By curating exclusively peaceful, articulate protesters and omitting scenes of barricade-breaking or stone-pelting that injured police officers (documented in Aaj Tak JXLIs0WtJAM [18:09--23:14]), the documentary constructs a pure moral contrast that leaves out the messy street dynamics at Parliament Street barricades.}
