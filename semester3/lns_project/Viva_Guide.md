## 3. Theoretical Foundations of the Five Shared Discourse Features

In a Language & Society viva, the professor will test whether you understand **why** these five features matter in sociolinguistics and Critical Discourse Analysis (Fairclough, van Dijk, Wodak, Halliday, Levinson):

### Feature 1: Naming (Referential Strategies — Reisigl & Wodak)
- **What it is:** How social actors and events are categorized lexically (nouns, epithets, metaphors). There is no "neutral" label when social conflict occurs—choosing one noun highlights certain moral traits and hides others.
- **Best Cross-Outlet Contrast in Your Project:**
  - **The 8 arrested Ayodhya men:** `"low-level functionaries, some cashiers"` (Republic TV `0H-bruoEkaY` `[03:33]`) vs `"bali ka bakra"` [scapegoat] for `"RSS VHP ka aadmi"` Champat Rai (Sarthak `lb7gZnyZH0c` `[00:46]`) vs `"temple-owners"` (Dhruv Rathee `oeH0fOSJuX8` `[07:33]`).
  - **The 20 July protesters vs attackers:**Notice the **complete mirror-image inversion of the word `"goons"`**:
    - Republic TV (`HTvb6E_1Qdc`, `[01:47]`, `[04:19]`) calls the *protesters* `"rabid, bloodthirsty mobs"` and `"goons of Abhijit Dipke"`.
    - The Wire (`pYaTVvHeGoM`, `[07:42]`, `[11:26]`) calls the *unbadged state-aligned assailants* `"an army of goons"` and `"paid lumpens"`, while calling protesters `"these kids"` / `"these bachas"` (`[23:18]`).
    - Aaj Tak (`JXLIs0WtJAM`) exhibits a **real-time lexical slide** inside a single 24-minute broadcast: `"pradarshankariyon"` (`[00:14]`) -> `"asamajik tatva"` (`[15:52]`) -> `"upadraviyon"` [rioters] (`[21:20]`, `[22:38]`).
    - Mohak Mangal (`v-RikSqb0Vw`) names them `"We the people of India..."` (`[Title]`).

### Feature 2: Presupposition (Pragmatics — Stalnaker / Levinson)
- **What it is:** Background assumptions built into the grammar of a sentence (via *wh*-questions, definite noun phrases, factive verbs like *prove/realize/regret*, temporal clauses, or change-of-state verbs) that remain true *even if the sentence is negated or answered either way*.
- **Best Examples to Quote in Viva:**
  1. **Factive verb (`proved`):** Republic TV (`0H-bruoEkaY`, `[03:22]`): *"they are questioning Yogi Adityanath **who's proved that** there is zero tolerance for any form of corruption."* (`prove` is factive -> presupposes zero tolerance is an established fact).
  2. **Why-question + temporal clause:** Sarthak Goswami (`lb7gZnyZH0c`, `[02:12–02:21]`): *"tum **sarapraizd kyon ho**? jab itne salon se **dharma ka dhandha ban gaya tha**..."* (presupposes religion had already become a business racket).
  3. **Ad-hominem *why*-question on Aaj Tak (`B4L1pz0o4dc`, `[26:52]`):** *"pran pratishtha ke taim... doori bana li thi. aaj chande ke mudde pe **kyon bhaee aapko Ram bhakton ki aastha ki duhaee dene lag gae**?"* (presupposes opposition concern is fake/opportunistic).
  4. **How-clause on Aaj Tak (`JXLIs0WtJAM`, `[21:20]`):** *"aanand mishra ne bataya ki **kaise upadraviyon ne police valon ko nishana banaya**"* (presupposes protesters were rioters who targeted police).
  5. **Negative-interrogative leading prompts on The Wire (`pYaTVvHeGoM`, `[22:40]`):** *"**Doesn't that suggest** that our political value system has degraded?"* (heavily constrains the interviewee toward agreement).

### Feature 3: Agency (Hallidayan Transitivity, Ergativity, Passivization & Nominalization)
- **What it is:** Who is cast as the active grammatical **Agent** (doing deliberate actions, often marked in Hindi with ergative `ne`) versus the passive **Patient**, or hidden entirely via **agentless passives** (`karvaee ki gaee`, `tiyar gais chhode gae`) and **nominalizations** (`comes a resignation`).
- **Best Examples to Quote in Viva:**
  1. **Aaj Tak's Passive Police vs Active Crowd (`JXLIs0WtJAM`, `[15:43]`):** *"us pradarshan ne ugra roop liya, pattharbaji huee aur uske baad **tiyar gais chhode gae**"* — Tear gas "was released" (agentless passive, Delhi Police deleted!) and sequenced *after* stone-pelting. Compare Sarthak (`EM1GeZufO6E`, `[03:39]`): *"dilli police aur sikyoriti phorses **ne** aam janta par lathi charj **kar diya**. pelet gans ki bauchhar **kar di**"* (explicit ergative `ne` + active compound verbs).
  2. **Republic TV's Agentless Resignation (`HTvb6E_1Qdc`, `[01:47]`):** *"...comes a resignation"* — When Dharmendra Pradhan resigns under CJP pressure, Arnab turns a forced ministerial exit into an agentless existential/motion noun phrase (`comes a resignation`) so he never has to make `CJP` the victorious subject or `Modi government` the yielding patient!
  3. **Dhruv Rathee's Inchoative Barricades (`l-6Pm-112io`, `[07:17]`):** *"by 10 am, **the barricades had begun to break**"* — Uses an inchoative verb (`begun to break`) as if welded iron barricades broke by themselves, softening protester force while assigning 100% active agency to `"The government started a war"` (`[02:19]`).
  4. **The Wire Turning Oversight into Intentional Design (`7rUL8fGgRJA`, `[24:44]`):** Where Aaj Tak's VHP guest calls lack of controls passive `"kuch avyavastha"` (`[34:21]`), Nilanjan Mukhopadhyay assigns purposeful agency: *"**they wanted** that it should not be a leak-proof system."*

### Feature 4: Antagonisation (Van Dijk's "Ideological Square" — Us vs Them)
- **What it is:** Teun van Dijk's **Ideological Square** states that partisan discourse systematically:
  1. Emphasizes **Our** good properties/actions,
  2. Emphasizes **Their** bad properties/actions,
  3. Mitigates **Our** bad properties/actions,
  4. Mitigates **Their** good properties/actions.
- **Sharpest Antagonisation Pair in the Entire Corpus (`"Ours"`):**
  - **Republic TV (`HTvb6E_1Qdc`, `[04:19–04:37]` & `[06:21–06:38]`):** Pits `"our brave soldiers... fighting terrorists in Anantnag"` and `"Nationalists"` against `"goons of Abhijit Dipke"`, ending with `"Nationalists, mobilize yourself... **This nation is ours**"` (exclusionary ideological ownership).
  - **The Wire (`pYaTVvHeGoM`, `[25:33–25:42]`) & Mohak Mangal (`v-RikSqb0Vw`, `[Title]`):** `"**We the people of India...**"` and `"**That house of parliament is ours. It belongs to the kids.**"` (inclusive constitutional ownership).

### Feature 5: Intensification & Softening (Boosting vs Hedging / Euphemism)
- **What it is:** Scaling epistemic certainty or emotional force **up** (hyperbole, repetition, visceral metaphors) or **down** (hedges like *may/reportedly/perhaps*, euphemisms like *informal/disarray/detain*).
- **Best Examples:**
  - **Softening/Euphemism for Your Own Side's Failures:**
    - VHP on Aaj Tak (`B4L1pz0o4dc`, `[34:21]`): `"kuch avyavastha huee hai"` [some disarray happened] for multi-crore temple theft.
    - Nripendra Misra on Aaj Tak (`[04:35]`): `"anaupcharik tarike se"` [in an informal manner].
    - Naseeruddin Shah (`pYaTVvHeGoM`, `[09:36]` & `[06:49]`): softens protester stone-pelting as `"even a rat fights back when it's pushed against the wall"` while hedging police casualties as `"supposedly more policemen injured"`.
  - **Intensification Against Opponents:**
    - Republic TV (`0H-bruoEkaY`, `[33:17–33:37]`): 5 consecutive rhetorical questions escalating Priyanka Kakkar's slip into `"PART OF THE TALIBAN SHOOTING SQUAD"`.
    - Dhruv Rathee (`l-6Pm-112io`, `[10:08]`, `[16:10]`): `"Jallianwala Bagh scene. General Dyer"`, `"East India Company... West India Company"`.
    - **Contrast with Mohak Mangal's Understated Restraint (`v-RikSqb0Vw`, `[02:06–02:39]`):** Mohak intensifies moral impact *by softening his own voice*—calmly reading dry government lathi-charge rules aloud right after a student describes broken legs and torn shoulders.

### Part II: Methodology, Transcription & Technical Pipeline Questions

**Q9. The instructions explicitly warned: *"Don't use AI dubbing—listen in the original language."* Why was that a trap on YouTube, and how did you prevent it?**
- **Answer:** In 2026, YouTube automatically attaches 15–20+ AI-dubbed audio and subtitle tracks (`en-US`, `ar`, `de-DE`, `fr-FR`, etc. on Hindi videos, and `hi` on English videos). If you naively ask a script for an `'en'` transcript on Sarthak Goswami or Mohak Mangal, YouTube returns the **AI-dubbed English translation**, which destroys original Hindi wording (`bali ka bakra`, `ghotala`, `upadravi`), flattens code-switching, and mistranslates idioms!
- In `scripts/fetch_transcripts.py`, `detect_original_spoken_language()` inspects `yt-dlp`'s stream metadata for the audio track tagged `"original"` (and not `"dubbed"`), locking Hindi creators (`Sarthak`, `Dhruv`, `Mohak`, `Aaj Tak`) strictly to native `hi` caption tracks and English channels (`Republic TV`, `The Wire`) strictly to native `en` tracks.

**Q11. Why did you write a custom Devanagari-to-Roman transliterator (`romanizer.py`) instead of just calling standard `ITRANS` or `IAST`?**
- **Answer:**
  1. Standard `ITRANS` outputs awkward mixed-case Sanskritized forms *without schwa deletion*—turning `राम मंदिर में सरकार` into `rAma maMdira meM sarakAra`!
  2. Academic `IAST` outputs non-ASCII diacritics (`rāma mandira mēṁ`), which are hard to search and unnatural for Hinglish news transcripts.
  3. Modern spoken Hindi deletes inherent schwa (`अ`) in two phonological environments (Ohala 1983; Pandey 1990): **word-finally** (`राम` -> `Ram`, not `Rama`) and **word-medially (`VCaCV -> VCCV`)** before long vowels (`सरकार` -> `sarkar`, `जनता` -> `janta`), while *retaining* final release schwa after consonant+glide/liquid clusters (`छात्र` -> `chhatra`, `सत्य` -> `satya`). Our `romanizer.py` implements these exact phonological rules, homorganic nasal assimilation (`ं` -> `m` before labials, `n` elsewhere), nukta normalization (`ज़` -> `z`, `फ़` -> `f`), acronym cleanup (`एफ़आईआर` -> `FIR`, `एसआईटी` -> `SIT`), and a hard regex guard guaranteeing **0 Devanagari characters (`U+0900..U+097F`)**.

**Q14. What can discourse analysis prove, and what *cannot* it prove from transcripts alone?**
- **Answer:** This is the heart of the *End-of-Event Summary*'s final prompt (*"What I cannot conclude from this transcript alone"*):
  - **What CDA CAN prove:** How an outlet constructs blame, legitimacy, agency, and in-group/out-group boundaries through verifiable linguistic choices (`abadakt` vs `diten`; `upadravi` vs `bachche`; active vs passive voice; who gets interviewed).
  - **What CDA CANNOT prove from a transcript alone:** Underlying forensic or judicial facts outside the text—such as whether Champat Rai personally pocketed stolen money, who parked the stone-filled trucks at `04:40 AM` on 20 July, or whether voters in UP will actually change their electoral vote in 2027. Keeping textual claims distinct from unverified external reality is what makes our analysis rigorous rather than partisan.
