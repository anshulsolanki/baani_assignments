2. Rule Patterns That Worked Exceptionally Well:
   - Central Nucleus Promotion (Rule 1) & Top-Level Depth (Rule 2): In exposition and argument paragraphs (Paragraphs 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, 9), the author states the central thesis in Sentence 1 and attaches the rest of the paragraph as an 'Elaboration (NS)' or 'Explanation (NS)' satellite. Following the all-Nucleus path from the root immediately identified the exact topic sentence in 9 out of 10 paragraphs.
   - Multinuclear Contrast & Joint Detection (Rule 4): In Paragraph 1 ('Contrast NN' between avoiding every popular issue and sticking to governmental structure) and Paragraph 8 ('Joint NN' linking ship remains in S1 and shipwreck contents in S2), rewarding high-level multinuclear relations captured both halves of the argument.
   - Satellite Pruning (Rule 5): Downweighting 'Background (SN)' and deep 'Elaboration (NS)' satellites successfully filtered out secondary example lists (such as the plant species list in Paragraph 3 S4 and the British-American word examples in Paragraph 5 S2-S3).

3. Failure Modes & Where Rule Patterns Struggled:
   - Narrative / Anecdotal Punchlines at Deep Leaves (Paragraphs 5 & 6): In joke or narrative-twist paragraphs (e.g., Paragraph 5's 'Ostentatious' punchline in S5, or Mother Teresa's final reply in Paragraph 6 S5), the parser attaches the dialogue progression as a chain of 'Elaboration (NS)' and 'Attribution' splits at depth 4-6. Because top-down RST rules favor shallow depth, the rules preferred the narrative setup over the deep punchline.
   - Meta-Discourse Introductory Sentences (Paragraph 10): In Paragraph 10, S1 ('This paper is part of an effort to relate various accounts') is the root Nucleus, while S2-S4 contain the actual comparison of RST and Systemic Linguistics. A pure tree-top rule picks S1 even though S1 is meta-discourse with low standalone informativeness.
   - Dangling Anaphoric Pronouns in Extractive Selection: Selecting S2 in Paragraph 10 ('It is the first in a pair...') or S2 in Paragraph 7 ('It ranks 184 urban areas...') without S1 leaves an unresolved pronoun ('It').

4. Concrete Ways to Improve the System:
   - Sub-Sentence EDU Compression: Instead of extracting whole sentences, prune Satellite EDUs (such as 'Attribution' or 'Background' clauses) inside a selected sentence to produce tighter, compressive summaries.
   - Genre-Aware Depth Weighting: Distinguish expository/argumentative texts (where top-level Nuclei hold the thesis) from narrative/dialogue texts (where 'Temporal' and climax leaves at the end of the chain hold the resolution).
   - Hybrid Lexical + Anaphora Post-Processing: Combine learned Logistic Regression RST weights (Extra Credit) with content-word TF-IDF salience and anaphora resolution (replacing dangling 'It/She' with its antecedent NP).
